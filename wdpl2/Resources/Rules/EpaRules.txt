International Eightball Pool Rules
Version 2c, May 2023
As endorsed by the IEPF and Ultimate Pool, and accepted by the EPA.

1. Spirit of the Game
International eight-ball pool is played as a gentleman's game. Players must accept all decisions made by the tournament officials. Disputes will be handled in accordance with the prescribed procedures.

2. Equipment and Table Layout
International eight-ball pool is played on a six-pocket rectangular table with six cushions.
Equipment permitted:
- A spotted white called a cue ball
- Two groups of object balls consisting of seven reds and seven yellows
- A striped eight-ball
- Spider, rest, gooseneck rest, cross rest
- Prescribed cues
The playing surface is the flat part of the table bordered by the cushions.
The surface must be marked with an eight-ball spot at the intersection of two imaginary diagonal lines joining the centre and corner pockets.
The surface must be marked with a 'rack line' - a 280mm vertical line going down through the eight-ball spot towards the top cushion. On the rack line must be two marker points, one for the head ball and the other for the black ball.
The cloth is marked with a break line parallel to the bottom cushion 1/5th of the length of the table away from the baulk cushion.
Using any equipment in a non-customary manner is never allowed and constitutes a foul.

3. The Object of the Game
The object of the game is to pot all the balls from your designated group (reds or yellows) and then legally pot the eight-ball to win the frame.

4. The Break
To commence a frame, the player who is breaking shall position the cue ball anywhere in baulk but MUST NOT position the cue ball over the break line by more than 50% of its diameter.
The Break will be deemed a 'Legal Break' if the player breaking obtains a minimum of three cumulative points:
- 1 point for each object ball potted (eight-ball included)
- 1 point for each object ball that has passed an imaginary line between the two centre pockets, that is not potted (a ball is deemed as having 'passed' if the whole ball is over the imaginary line)
Failure to perform a legal break MUST result in a re-rack. The opponent has the option to break or to choose to give the break back to the original breaker.
Groups are not decided on the break. The table remains open after the break.
If the eight-ball is potted off the break, it is always re-spotted after all balls have come to rest. The centre point of the eight-ball is placed on the eight-ball spot or if this is not available as near as possible to the spot in a direct line between the spot and the centre of the top cushion.
If the cue ball goes in-off on a legal break, the incoming player is awarded one visit with cue ball in hand, to be played from baulk, in any direction.
If the cue ball leaves the playing surface on a legal break, a standard foul will be called against the breaker, with the incoming player receiving one visit, played from anywhere on the playing surface.

5. The Rack
Object balls are racked with the head ball on the head-ball marker on the rack line. The eight-ball is placed on the eight-ball marker. The remaining balls are placed randomly in the rack, with reds and yellows distributed evenly.

6. Deciding Groups
Groups are decided on the first ball legally potted. The table is considered an open table when the choice of groups has not yet been determined. The table is always open immediately after the break shot. Balls potted from the break are disregarded.
If one of each group is potted in one shot, the player will be 'on' the group struck by the cue ball first unless a foul has occurred, in which case the table remains open.
Where the choice of group is not obvious (e.g. player cues up to a group of object balls, or both groups sitting close together, or snookered behind the eight-ball), the player MUST indicate to the referee and/or the opponent the intended group.
- Failure to call a group after potting a ball whilst the table is open is loss of turn; the table remains open.
- Failure to strike called group is loss of turn; the table remains open.
- Striking a called group but potting the other group is loss of turn; the table remains open.
- Potting a called group is a legal pot and the group is assigned to the striker.
Groups are not decided on a foul shot.

7. Continuing a Frame
If a legal shot is played and balls from the player's own group are potted, play continues and the player is entitled to an additional shot. The player will lose their turn and return control of the table to the opponent if they:
- Fail to pot one or more of their own group, AND/OR
- Commit a standard foul

8. Combination Shots
Object balls from both groups can be potted without penalty in a single 'combination' shot. Combination shots do not have to be called. The potted balls may drop into the pockets in any order but the 'on' ball must be struck legally first. Attempting to complete a combination shot that results in only potting a ball not 'on' is LOSS OF TURN.

9. Loss of Turn
As long as the player strikes an 'on' ball first, should an opponent's ball be potted without also potting an 'on' ball, it will be loss of turn. Control of the table returns to the opponent; the cue ball is played from where it lies.
Examples:
- Potting an opponent's ball accidentally
- Failing to complete a legal combination shot
- A tactical shot e.g. playing a designated group legally to pot an opponent's ball
- Failure to call a group after potting a ball whilst the table is 'open', if the group was not obvious

10. Standard Fouls
Standard fouls shall be called as soon as they occur. The incoming player is awarded one visit and cue ball in hand, which can be played from its current position or placed anywhere on the playing surface.
Standard fouls include:
- Potting the cue ball (in-off), except off the break
- Playing from outside baulk when obliged to play from baulk
- Playing a shot immediately after playing a foul or after the referee has called a foul (playing out of turn, including breaking out of turn)
- Accidentally striking the cue ball with any part of the cue other than the tip
- Accidentally striking any ball other than the cue ball with any part of the cue or cue tip
- Playing a shot before all balls have come to rest from the previous shot
- Playing a shot before any balls that require re-spotting have been re-spotted
- Coaching: during a frame, a player must play without receiving advice from other persons. If a team member or supporter offers advice, the referee will issue a first and final warning; a repetition results in a standard foul
- Leaving the playing area without permission (timeout must be granted by the referee)
- Playing a 'Push Shot' (tip remains in contact with the cue ball once it has commenced forward motion)
- Double-hitting the cue ball
- Failing to perform a 'Legal Shot'
- Playing a shot while not having at least one foot touching the floor
- Failing to play away from touching ball(s)
- A ball that remains off the playing surface
- A player's body, clothing, jewellery or accessory coming into contact with any ball
- Jump shot: if the cue ball leaves the bed of the playing surface and does not strike an object ball it would have struck had the cue ball not left the surface
- Marking the table to assist a shot
- Time foul

11. Loss of Frame Fouls
The following result in loss of the frame:
- Deliberately not playing a ball from their own group first, or an on ball after a foul
- Unsporting behaviour / bringing the game into disrepute, including: a second offence for foul language (verbal warning for first offence), throwing a cue around / unscrewing as if to concede, continually arguing with the referee or opponent, interfering when the opponent is on the shot either verbally or physically
- Committing a foul in the same shot that the eight-ball is potted (except on the break)
- Potting the eight-ball when balls of the player's own group are still on the table (except on the break), including potting the last group ball and eight-ball in the same shot
- A player who clearly and intentionally fails to make an attempt to play a ball of their own group
- Potting the eight-ball legally, then proceeding to move the remaining balls before they have come to rest
- Deliberately striking the cue ball with any part of the cue other than the tip
- Deliberately striking any ball other than the cue ball with any part of the cue or cue tip
- Deliberately moving balls that were not played as part of a shot

12. Stalemate
If any situation arises whereby a 'legal shot' cannot be played, it is a player's responsibility to ask for a stalemate. If the referee (or opponent if no referee) confirms a stalemate, the frame shall be restarted with a re-rack. The player who originally broke will restart the re-racked frame. A referee will disregard a player's ability when making a stalemate decision.

13. Frozen Balls
An object ball is deemed frozen whilst touching a cushion. It is a standard foul if the cue ball initially contacts a frozen ball first and the shot does not result in:
- A ball being potted, OR
- The cue ball contacting a cushion, OR
- The frozen ball contacting a cushion attached to a different rail, OR
- Any other object ball contacting a cushion with which it was not already in contact before the shot is played
A ball is only deemed frozen if confirmed by the referee or player prior to the shot being taken.

14. Outside Interference
There is no penalty if balls are moved by persons other than players taking part in the frame, as a result of players being bumped, or events deemed not within a player's control. The referee will return the balls as close as possible to their original positions. Re-racks will only be granted in extreme circumstances.

15. Shot Clock
Each player is allotted 45 seconds for each shot. The tip of the cue must have made contact with the cue ball within this time or a standard foul shall be granted to the opponent. The 45-second shot clock will commence once all balls come to rest. The timekeeper will verbally count down from 5 seconds.
One extension per frame is permitted per player, whereby the referee will add 15 seconds to the remaining time at the request of the player.

16. Balls Falling into a Pocket
A shot is 'complete' when all balls have come to rest.
After a shot is 'complete', any balls that drop into a pocket without being struck will be replaced to their original positions.
During a shot 'in progress', any balls that drop into a pocket without being struck and wouldn't have been played as part of a shot shall be replaced to their original positions after all balls have come to rest.
During a shot 'in progress', any balls that drop into a pocket without being struck and would have been played as part of a shot - in this instance ALL BALLS will be replaced to their original positions after all balls have come to rest.

17. Balls off the Playing Surface
It is a standard foul if a ball leaves the playing surface at any point during the frame and does not return by its own means and remains off the playing surface (other than being potted).
If it is the cue ball, then it is ball in hand, to be played anywhere on the table.
Object balls are re-spotted on the eight-ball spot or as near as possible to that spot in a direct line between the spot and the centre point of the top cushion.
Object balls are re-spotted in the following order: eight-ball, then red, then yellow.

18. Players' Responsibility
It is the responsibility of each player to be aware of all rules. A referee on request may advise on the rules of the game but must not provide any subjective opinion that would affect the result of the frame. Players may always call for rule clarification during play; however, this is not considered as a timeout.

19. Touching Balls
A player must play away from ALL touching balls at an angle of perpendicular (90 degrees) or greater and fulfil the requirements of a 'legal shot'. A player shall not be penalised if playing away from touching balls causes any touching ball to rock or move slightly because it was resting on the cue ball.
If the table is open and touching balls have been confirmed, the player must nominate a group (for clarity of the shot, not to decide a group for the remainder of the frame) and perform all the requirements of a legal shot.
If the cue ball is touching an 'on' ball, the player is deemed to have made initial contact in order to fulfil the requirements of a legal shot.

20. Legal Shot
To play a 'legal shot' the player must cause the cue ball's initial contact to be with a ball 'on' and THEN must either:
- Pot any ball or balls (except the eight-ball if not ball 'on'), OR
- Cause the cue ball or any object ball to contact a cushion

21. Simultaneous Contact
Simultaneous contact of object balls is allowed as long as one of the contacted object balls is 'on'.

22. Completion of a Frame
A frame is won when a player legally pots the eight-ball after all balls of their designated group have been potted.
